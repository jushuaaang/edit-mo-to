# Questions while studying the brief (short design)

**Why:** a brief rule can be unclear, implied, conflicting or missing. Today the step 2 checklist then holds a guess, and QA checks against the guess.

**Goal:** step 2 asks about those rules before anything is written.

**Non-goals:** no new gate; answers are not saved to client notes; steps 3-12 unchanged.

**Anti-goal:** a long interview on a clear brief.

**Approaches:**
1. Questions inside the existing step 2 ⛔ gate, right after the checklist. **Picked:** same gate count, one stop, answers land on the checklist.
2. A separate gate before the checklist. Rejected: adds a stop and changes the gate list.
3. Style or script agent asks later. Rejected: questions scattered across steps, and QA has already used the guesses.

**Behaviour:**
- While building the checklist, mark rules that are unclear, implied ("premium", "like the reference"), conflicting (with each other or with the editor's preferences), or missing when the brief is silent on length, ratio, music, CTA or on-screen text.
- At the existing step 2 gate, after the checklist: "Questions about the brief", at most 6, most important first, each with one line on what it changes.
- Each answer becomes a checklist row, source "editor, <date>"; QA checks it like any brief rule.
- A clear brief: "No questions about the brief." and the gate runs as today.

**Left to the builder:** exact wording; ordering by how much each answer changes the build.
